The FIFA World Cup 2026 Master Dataset: A 12-Entity Relational Benchmark of the 48-Team Expanded Tournament

Mominul Islam1,*
1Department of Data Science & Quantitative Analytics, Open Sports Data Initiative, Dhaka, Bangladesh.
*e-mail: mominul.ptr@gmail.com | GitHub: github.com/mominullptr/FIFA-World-Cup-2026-Dataset | Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21566741
Abstract
The 2026 FIFA World Cup introduced a historic structural expansion in international football, transitioning to a 48-team format comprising 104 matches across 16 host venues in Canada, Mexico, and the United States. Despite the surge in quantitative sports modeling, open-access sports repositories frequently suffer from unvalidated relational keys, missing substitution timelines, or lack of expected goals (xG) metrics. Here, we present the FIFA World Cup 2026 Master Dataset, a complete open-access 3rd Normal Form (3NF) relational database covering all 104 matches, 48 qualified national teams, 1,248 registered squad players, 2,704 minute-by-minute lineup records, tactical team statistics, expected goals (xG), and engineered predictive features. Data was harvested continuously across the tournament using an automated extraction pipeline, mapped into 12 normalized relational entities, and validated using an automated 9-stage programmatic verification framework. Delivered in CSV, Apache Parquet, and SQLite formats (sqlite_fifa_world_cup_2026.db), the dataset provides a standardized open-access benchmark for sports analytics, computational modeling, and format expansion analysis.

Background & Summary

Quantitative football analytics has experienced exponential growth over the past decade, driven by advanced spatial tracking and fine-grained event metrics [1]. However, open-access research remains severely constrained by commercial paywalls. Proprietary vendors such as Opta, StatsBomb, and Wyscout restrict raw match feeds behind expensive enterprise licenses, leaving independent researchers and academic institutions with fragmented, unverified data summaries [2].

The 2026 FIFA World Cup presented a transformative structural shift in international tournament dynamics. As the first 48-team expanded tournament, it featured 104 matches played across 16 venues spanning sea-level stadiums to high-altitude venues (e.g., Estadio Azteca at 2,240 meters elevation) [3]. Analyzing team fatigue, squad rotation efficiency, and referee strictness under this expanded structure requires a unified relational schema.

To resolve these accessibility barriers, we developed the FIFA World Cup 2026 Master Dataset. The dataset normalizes team demographics, player bio-metrics, tactical statistics, expected goals (xG), and match lineups into an open-access, fully validated relational database architecture.

Methods & Data Ingestion

Data collection was executed continuously from June 11 through July 19, 2026, leveraging automated Python extraction pipelines querying official FIFA match centers, Sofascore API endpoints, Transfermarkt player valuations, and geographic survey records.

Data Processing Engine

Raw JSON responses were parsed, disambiguated, and reconciled using integer primary key mappings. Expected goals (xG) for each team performance were computed by aggregating shot-level probability vectors:

xGteam = ∑i=1..S E[ Goali | xi, yi, θi ] (1)

where xi, yi denote spatial shot coordinates, and θi represents shot angle and body part indicators. Pre-match Elo ratings Ri and rolling team form metrics were computed to derive feature vectors for match outcome prediction:

ΔElom = Rhome - Raway + γhost (2)
Formi(t) = ⅓ ∑j=1..3 ( GFi, t-j - GAi, t-j ) (3)

Technical Validation

To ensure zero-error relational integrity, the dataset was submitted to an automated 9-stage programmatic verification engine (validate_dataset.py).

Key validation rules enforced include:

  1. Squad Completeness: Verified that ∑p = 26 players for each of the 48 qualified nations (∑ = 1,248).
  2. Lineup Starter Assertion: Verified that every completed match contains exactly 11 starting XI players per team (∑s = 11).
  3. Possession Bound Verification: Asserted that home, away, and contested ball possession sum to valid range [80%, 100%].
  4. Goalkeeper Constraint Assertion: Validated that saves, clean sheets, and goals conceded are populated exclusively for goalkeepers (position == 'GK').

Usage Notes & Code Benchmarks

The relational SQLite database (sqlite_fifa_world_cup_2026.db) allows direct integration with SQL analytical engines, R, Python, and Power BI.

SQL Relational Benchmark Query

SELECT 
    p.player_name, 
    t.team_name, 
    ps.goals, 
    ps.minutes_played,
    ROUND(CAST(ps.minutes_played AS FLOAT) / ps.goals, 1) AS mins_per_goal
FROM player_stats ps
JOIN squads_and_players p ON ps.player_id = p.player_id
JOIN teams t ON ps.team_id = t.team_id
WHERE ps.goals > 0
ORDER BY ps.goals DESC, mins_per_goal ASC
LIMIT 10;
